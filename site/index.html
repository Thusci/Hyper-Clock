Hyper Clock turns a ten-year-old Windows tablet into a 24/7 desk display. It's a quiet, hotel-lobby watch face with the weather, the light outside, today's timetable and every device at home, plus a phone-friendly Home Assistant console and a secure control panel behind it.

What's inside

Four parts, one quiet object.

The display holds no secrets. A single control plane talks to Home Assistant over one WebSocket, and the tablet reports on itself through a small sidecar.

Watch face

Information first, never busy

Clock, weather HUD, today's classes on a timeline, calendar, personal devices with battery rings, room climate and power, all laid out in the restraint of a watch catalogue.

Multigraph

Three hands, three readings

A minimal mode inspired by Ferrari's Luce Multigraph. The same hands re-arrange into the time, a wind compass, or sunrise and sunset on a 24-hour scale, moving along smootherstep curves.

WebGL sky

The weather, outside the window

One shader for daylight, clouds, rain, snow, fog and lightning, plus a Starlight-style canopy on clear nights with the occasional shooting star.

HA console

Every room, from any phone

A mobile-first control panel for lights, switches, scenes, automations, sensors and cameras. Rooms, favourites and search, with subscriptions limited to what's on screen.

Back-stage

One secure boundary

The only holder of the HA token. LAN-only by default, with session, CSRF and rate limits on every write, revocable sessions, an audit log, and optional authenticator-app sign-in.

Q775 sidecar

The tablet reports on itself

A Python daemon that sends CPU, temperature, battery, ambient light and power to Home Assistant, and switches the backlight, sleep and wake.

Maison design language

Four daylight phases.

Warm neutrals, one champagne-gold accent and hairline borders. The whole interface follows the real sun, as reported by Home Assistant, with a local solar calculation as fallback. This page follows yours.

Architecture

Secrets stay on the server.

The kiosk reads a one-way event stream; commands go through a server-side relay that adds the kiosk secret. Phones use same-origin sessions. Only Nginx faces the network.

Install

Two commands on the device.

Ubuntu with systemd, Nginx and an Openbox/Chromium kiosk on tty1. No Docker required, though a Compose setup is included. Both scripts are safe to re-run.

Prepare

Install Node.js 24, Nginx, Xorg, Openbox, Chromium and CJK fonts, and create the service account. Then put two secrets and the HA token in /etc/hyper-clock/.

# secrets: one each
openssl rand -hex 32
openssl rand -hex 32

Deploy

Backs up runtime data, syncs to /opt/hyper-clock, rebuilds only what changed, and installs the systemd units and Nginx site.

sudo scripts/deploy.sh

Launch

Starts each service in order and waits for its health check, then hands the screen to the kiosk.

sudo scripts/launch.sh \
  --takeover-display

Full guide, troubleshooting and sign-in options are in the deployment guide and the 中文技术手册.
